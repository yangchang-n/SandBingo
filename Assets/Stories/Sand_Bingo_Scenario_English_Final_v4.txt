You don't realize which goodbye was the last until long afterward.

For decades, this land had been nearly bone-dry, and familiar faces slipped away like sand through open fingers.

Piper was the brightest of them all, and the biggest dreamer.

She was always talking, so the well had no right to be this quiet without her.

She was already gone. In the end, all that remained was her voice, lingering somewhere deep within.

The one left behind would carry that voice for years and years, until one day even that—

Piper : ...What are you even talking about?

Piper : I'm right here, you know? ...Did the heat finally get to her?

Piper stood there with an empty bucket in one hand, looking utterly baffled.

Tessa : Oh. You're back.

Tessa : Faster than I expected. You could've taken your time.

Piper : Taken my time? What, so you could finish my funeral first?

Tessa : ...So how was the well over there? Any sign of water?

Piper : Seriously... That one's hopeless too. How about yours?

Tessa : Nothing yet, but look here. Feels like we could get something flowing if we hit it just right, doesn't it?

Piper : Oh, you're right. So why weren't you working on it?

Tessa : You're better at this than I am these days. I was waiting for our ace.

Piper : You just couldn't be bothered...

-----

Piper : As you know, waking a well takes a special, limited resource called "Oasis Sand."

Tessa : But why do we need sand, of all things, to wake a well? I still don't get it.

Piper : ...You've been doing this for three years, and you're asking that now?

Piper : It gives the parched ground a jolt so the water deep underground can find a way up...

Piper : I mean... something like that? That's roughly how it works, right?

Tessa : That's pretty vague. I still don't get it.

Piper : Anyway, you can only drop Oasis Sand inside this area here.

Piper : There's a limit to how much you can use at once, so use it carefully, a little at a time!

Tessa : Right. Managing how much you use is the whole trick. You're much better at that than I am.

Piper : Once you've used a certain amount of Oasis Sand, mud starts building up in response.

Tessa : Mud is a good sign. It means the ground's getting wet. But let too much build up in one spot, and it'll clog the well right back up.

Piper : Exactly! So we need to link the Oasis Sand together before the mud links up.

Piper : Line up five or more squares horizontally, vertically, or diagonally,

Piper : and you'll give the ground enough of a jolt before the mud can build up!

-----

Tessa : Water's starting to come up. That should do it.

Tessa : But horizontal, vertical... wait. "Squares"? What squares?

Piper : Who knows? Hehe. Maybe I can only see them in my mind's eye.

Tessa : Yeah, of course. No wonder you're so good at this when you've barely been at it.

Tessa : Geniuses really are in a league of their own, I swear...

Piper : Come on, let's go. We need to find another well we can wake before evening!

Tessa : Yeah, yeah... Must be nice having that much energy to spare.

Tessa : Hm?

Piper : What is it?

Tessa : Nothing. Let's go.

-----

Piper : It's hot.

Tessa : Yeah... Feels like it gets even hotter as the sun goes down.

Piper : Why do the people who wake wells have to be thirstier than everyone else?

Tessa : No idea. Too hot to think about complicated things.

Piper : That's so unfair... Now I don't even feel like working.

Tessa : Want to hear a joke so bad it'll give you chills?

Piper : I'm already getting annoyed, so don't.

Tessa : All right. How about this, then?

Tessa : Wake the next well and I'll give you a present.

Piper : No thanks. Not interested.

Piper : ...What is it, though?

Tessa : Hmm... Honestly, I'm not really sure myself.

Piper : That's not how presents work.

Piper : Don't tell me it's just a day off or something.

Tessa : That's not it.

Piper : Then it's not going to be something like our friendship or the satisfaction of a job well done, right?

Tessa : Well... when you put it that way, you're not far off...

Tessa : W-Wait! Wait up for meee...!

-----

Piper : It's coming up, Tessa! We've got water!

Tessa : Whew... That one was a little rough, huh?

Piper : Yeah. I was this close to giving up.

Tessa : Thanks for sticking with it. Have some water and rest for a bit.

Piper : You too, Tessa. Nice work.

Piper : Hey, aren't you forgetting something?

Tessa : Ah... right.

Tessa : But it's not something I can exactly hand you.

Tessa : It's not something you can carry around.

Piper : This is sounding more suspicious by the second.

Tessa : I know a place. It's a bit of a walk, though...

Piper : Whew... Then let me rest a little longer before we head out.

-----

Tessa : Haaah... I could've sworn it was around here...

Piper : How much longer am I supposed to just follow you around? The sun's already down.

Piper : What's with all these weeds...?

Tessa : Dried flower stalks. They used to bloom wherever water ran underground.

Tessa : If I remember right, there's a really good well somewhere around here.

Tessa : Once you wake it, it'll gush for ages.

Tessa : But it's a tough one to work on, so...

Piper : So you've just left it alone ever since it stopped flowing.

Tessa : Yeah... But today feels like the day to finally take it on.

Piper : Getting you to finally tackle a chore you've been putting off...

Piper : Maybe this was really a present for you, Tessa?

Tessa : Well, thanks for the little push. Really...

Piper : Oh! Is that it?

Tessa : Ah, good eye. Yeah, that's the one.

Piper : Wow. That really doesn't look easy.

Tessa : But if we pull it off, this whole area should have a reliable water source for a while.

Tessa : Ready to get started?

-----

Piper : Pwah! There it is! It's coming out!!

Piper : Whoa, careful! I didn't think it'd gush out like this...!

Tessa : Whew... Nice work. Well, I'm soaked.

Piper : That was really tough. Aaah... At least it's nice and cool.

Tessa : Yeah... after being hot and exhausted all day.

Tessa : Right now... this really is satisfying.

Piper : I like it. Not quite what I expected, but...

Piper : Whoa, look at the stars. There are so many tonight.

Piper : Doesn't it look like someone scattered glittering sand across the sky?

Tessa : With that much sand, we could probably wake a well in the sky too.

Piper : Then it'd be nice if this "rain" thing fell, even just once.

That night, it was hard to tell above from below.

Water sprang up beneath their feet, and sand glittered overhead.

Someday, many years from now, this night would come back to mind.

By then, it would be clear what had begun that day.

And the end of that story would probably—

Piper : Okay, that's enough! The end!
